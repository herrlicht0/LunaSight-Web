Why LunaSight?

The problem we set out to solve

At the lunar south pole, nearby landing sites and different arrival dates can have very different sunlight and direct-to-Earth communication opportunities. Finding those trade-offs often means moving between specialist tools and interpreting data that is difficult to compare quickly, especially for people new to mission planning.

Our approach

Team Cosmic Variance built LunaSight as an approachable visual workspace for exploring candidate sites and mission times together. The panorama and horizon view show where the Sun and Earth sit relative to the local horizon; the interactive Moon makes locations tangible; and the timeline and telemetry help reveal how lighting and communication conditions change. Our aim is to make the trade-offs easier to explore and explain—not to replace detailed mission analysis.

How to use the program

  1. Choose a place. Use SELECT SITE, enter latitude and longitude and choose CHECK COORDINATES, or drag the 3D Moon to rotate it and click a visible point. The selected coordinates appear below the globe.
  2. Choose a time. Set the mission epoch and move the timeline slider to explore conditions at different times. Select a 24-hour or lunar-day span to change the displayed analysis window.
  3. Read the sky. Drag the panorama or use the left and right arrow keys to look around. The Sun and Earth markers show their modeled directions; the dashed terrain profile and horizon help you judge whether each body is above local terrain.
  4. Compare constraints. Check the sunlight and direct-to-Earth communication metrics, their window percentages, and the assessment in Telemetry. Change the DSN profile to explore the link estimate. You can move or minimize the planner, telemetry, and Moon panels.
  5. Optional data and sound. Load a supported JPL Horizons data file through the planner to use its samples. Turn on COMMS CHANNEL for background music and radio/static that respond to the modeled link quality.

Important: This challenge demo uses an analytic ephemeris by default and illustrative horizon/panorama information, not a complete high-resolution terrain model. Its estimates are for exploration and education—not landing certification or operational decisions.
